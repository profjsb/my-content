In this 14-minute a16z interview, filmed at the firm's second annual Academic Roundtable (Menlo Park, September 2014) and published in January 2015, Bloom, then a UC Berkeley astronomy professor and co-founder/CTO of Wise.io, presents machine learning as a way to approximate human intuition once there is too much data for people to inspect. He describes real-bogus classification of image-subtraction candidates (about a thousand bogus for every real one) feeding a second layer of algorithms that decides what kind of transient each real one is; why time-series data pushed his group to hire statisticians and computer scientists and seeded statistics PhDs; astronomy's long habit of pulling on industry (Galileo's telescope, CCDs at Bell Labs); and how the pipeline caught a nearby Type Ia supernova (SN 2011fe) about 11 hours after explosion, days earlier than any before it, enabling worldwide follow-up and several Nature papers. He then carries that production know-how over to Wise.io's work on churn and customer interactions: actionable, probabilistic insight; personalized models, from spam filters to Gmail's explained categories; and a future in which companies buy products with machine intelligence baked in rather than build their own data-science teams.
