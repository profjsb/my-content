The inaugural UC Berkeley Data Science Lecture (June 21, 2013), introduced by Fernando Pérez: Bloom asks whether "data science" is an intellectual field or a toolkit, casting himself (and Galileo) as data-driven scientists. He tours time-domain problems where astronomers, statisticians, and computer scientists meet: hunting Earth analogs in Kepler's parts-per-million light curves, separating quasars from stars by modeling their variability as a damped random walk (with Nat Butler), classifying variable stars across a messy taxonomy when classifiers trained on one survey fail on another (James Long's thesis), and machine-learned discovery and follow-up of transients. He closes with a "novelty squared" challenge: the best collaborations are novel for both the domain science and the methods. A panel with Philip Stark, Michael Silver, and Richard Allen compares seismology, neuroscience, and astronomy data, campus silos, and data science as a "union of intersections"; the audience Q&A turns to training students, the Python boot camp, and a new reproducible and collaborative data science course.
