In this April 2019 BIDS Data Science Lecture, Bloom frames astrophysical machine learning within the democratization of data, compute, and algorithms, arguing that the scientific winners will be teams pairing deep domain expertise with data-science fluency and diverse skills. He walks through ML across the time-domain workflow: real-bogus discovery in the Palomar Transient Factory that caught SN 2011fe within hours of explosion and constrained its white-dwarf progenitor; Michael Medford's Planet 9 search, where a millisecond-latency classifier in Docker containers at NERSC decides which of order a trillion candidate detections to keep; just-in-time inference written into collaboration tools and the open-source SkyPortal; a probabilistic variable-star catalog mined for rare DY Per stars and eclipsing binaries; stellar parameters inferred from light curves; and the Cesium toolkit. He closes with neural-net work (a U-Net cosmic-ray cleaner for Hubble images, autoencoding RNNs whose bottleneck layers recover physical parameters, semi-supervised anomaly detection, and GANs for simulation and parameter estimation) and an invitation to collaborate.
