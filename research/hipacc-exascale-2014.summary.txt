In this 18-minute talk at UC-HiPACC's "Approaching Exascale" workshop (LBNL, March 2014), Bloom tours the computational side of data-driven astronomical inference: a 307-parameter Bayesian fit of RR Lyrae period-luminosity relations across 13 bandpasses (six days per MCMC chain) that yields ~1% distances and 3D dust tomography of the Galaxy; a fully Bayesian image-registration and astrometry package using Gaussian processes for local distortion fields; machine-learned probabilistic classification of 50,000 variable stars into the browsable bigmacc.info catalog, used to double the known R Coronae Borealis and DY Persei stars; predicting stellar temperature, gravity, and metallicity from light curves alone; and real-time real-bogus and transient classification in the Palomar Transient Factory, where a robot now takes part in discovery. He closes on the "peopleware" gap across the time-domain sciences and an NSF BIGDATA grant to build an open-source machine-learning toolkit.
